The Handstand Project

A wall-based progression to a freestanding handstand. A short daily mobility practice, with skill work added 2 to 3 days a week, over months.

Daily 10–15 min Skill 2–3×/wk 4 phases

Two tiers, so it stays manageable. Daily practice (~10–15 min, six days): the mobility and wrist routine, your minimum. Skill top-up (2–3 days when you have time): your current phase. Strength stays in the gym. Each exercise has its own ▷ Watch demo; advance a phase only when you pass its test.

How the week works

Every day (aim for six): the Daily practice below, about 10–15 minutes. That alone is real progress, it builds the wrist and shoulder mobility you are missing.

2 to 3 days, when you have time: add the Skill top-up (another 10–15 minutes). Fit it around your lifting, and take at least one full rest day whenever suits.

Daily practice · ~10–15 min

Six days a week, on its own. This is the habit, and it builds the wrist and shoulder mobility a handstand needs. The ▷ videos are to learn a move or go deeper on the mobility; you do not need to play them in full every day, just do the doses.

  • Wrist flexor stretch (fingers back) 30–60sKneel, palms down and fingers pointing back, lean gently to stretch the forearm flexors.▷ Watch
  • Wrist rocks, all directions 3×10On all fours: rock forward, back with fingers turned in, then side to side. Slow.▷ Watch
  • Finger pulses ×10–15Press through the fingertips and lift the palm slightly, rhythmically.▷ Watch
  • Wall slides 2×10Back to a wall, slide the arms overhead and down keeping contact. Can't keep contact? That is the mobility to build, and the video is a full progressive routine for it.▷ Watch
  • Band pass-throughs 2×10Hold a band wide, take it overhead and behind, and back. Widen the grip if the shoulders pinch.▷ Watch
  • Scapular push-ups ×8In a plank, spread the shoulder blades, then squeeze.▷ Watch
  • Hollow hold 2×10sTucked, lower back flat. Skip this on skill top-up days, the skill work covers your core.▷ Watch

Skill top-up · 2–3 days

On days you have more time, do your Daily practice first, then open the phase you are on and do its skill and wrist work.

0 FoundationsWrists, shoulders, core · 2–4 weeks ▸

Goal: build wrist tolerance, open shoulders overhead, and a hollow-body core before inverting.

Skill

  • Quadruped wrist load (fingers forward) 3×10On all fours, fingers forward, rock the weight forward over the hands to end range and back, slow, pausing briefly at the end. Lean further over time. Stop at sharp pain.▷ Watch
  • Hollow-body hold 4×20–30sLower back flat. Start tucked; extend the limbs only while the back stays flat.▷ Watch
  • Back-to-wall shoulder flexion 3×8–10Back to a wall, reach the arms overhead to touch it and lower, without arching the back or bending the elbows.▷ Watch
  • Wall walk 3–5 repsFeet on the wall, walk the hands in towards the wall and back down. Build towards walking closer to vertical.▷ Watch

Wrists

  • Wrist curls 2×15–20Light dumbbell or band, palms up, curl the wrists up and lower slowly. Pain-free and high-rep.▷ Watch
  • Wrist extensions 2×15–20Same, palms down (reverse curls). Builds the other side of the forearm, the bit machine lifting misses.▷ Watch
  • Fingertip plank hold build to 3×20sTake weight through spread fingertips and hold as long as your form lasts, then rest. Too hard? Start on your knees, or in a quadruped fingertip hold, and build up.▷ Watch

Ready for Phase 2

2 Kick-up & bailIn, and safely out · 4–8+ weeks ▸

Goal: learn to enter a freestanding handstand, and to exit it. Learn the bail first.

Bail first

Spend the first week or two on the cartwheel bail alone, until it is automatic in both directions, before any freestanding kick-ups. Clear about 2m by 2m with nothing to hit.

Skill

  • Cartwheel bail drill 5 each sideWhen tipping past balance, turn the shoulders and cartwheel out to the side. Drill it deliberately.▷ Watch
  • Back-to-wall kick-up 5–8 repsLunge, hands a hand-span from the wall, kick up so the heels meet the wall softly.▷ Watch
  • Toe pulls 5–8 repsFrom chest-to-wall with toes touching, push tall and lean the shoulders forward just enough to float the toes off. Find the balance point on the wall first.▷ Watch
  • Kick-up to balance (off wall) 5–8 repsIn your cleared space, kick up, catch a moment of balance, bail out the side when it drifts.▷ Watch

Milestones

Reference

Alignment · the shape you are chasing

Stack the big joints, wrists under shoulders under hips under ankles, so you balance with little effort. The common fault is arching the lower back (the banana), which pushes the hips out of the stack. What each part does, from the ground up:

  • HandsFingers spread, gripping the floor, weight through the fingertips.
  • ShouldersPush tall and reach fully overhead, ears between the arms. This is what stacks you.
  • RibsDo not let your ribcage pop up and forwards. Gently brace your stomach so the front of your body stays flat. Same feeling as the hollow-body hold.
  • HipsDirectly over your shoulders, not your backside sticking out behind you. Tuck your tailbone slightly under, the way you would to flatten your lower back on the floor.
  • LegsGlutes squeezed, legs together and straight, toes pointed.
Guardrails · stop and read if something feels off

Wrists: sharp or joint pain

Forearm ache is fine. Sharp pain, or pain in the joint, means stop. Go back to the warm-up and prep-only loading, and cut volume next session.

If the wrist angle hurts

Reduce the angle: work on parallettes or push-up handles, or make fists on a mat. Progress back to flat palms over weeks.

Neck or arms: tingling, or pressure in the neck

You should feel it in the hands and shoulders. Tingling down the arms or a compressed neck means come down.

Shoulders: pinch at the top of the reach

Do not force it. Reduce the range and do more shoulder mobility. It opens over weeks.

Head rush or dizziness

Come out of every inversion slowly. Sit and hydrate if light-headed.

Pinch in the lower back upside down

You are arching (the banana). Ribs down, glutes on, more hollow work.

Clear the space before kicking up

From Phase 2 on, clear about 2m by 2m with nothing to hit, ideally over a mat or carpet, and bail towards the open side.

Fear when tipping over

Never kick up freestanding until the cartwheel bail is automatic. That is the job of Phase 2.
