A build-towards-it programme

The Handstand Project

A progressive, under-20-minutes routine that treats the handstand not as a trick to rush, but as the reason to build your wrists, shoulders, core and back. Six short mornings a week, over months, with a clear test before you move up.

6 mornings + 1 rest ≤ 20 min a day 4 phases, self-paced Wrist-first

Read this once before you start

This page is yours. Each morning, open the one phase you are on, do the warm-up plus that phase's work, and close it. Do not skip ahead. You move up only when you can tick every box in that phase's Ready when… test, honestly. The ticks save on this device, so your progress is here when you come back.

Progress is measured in months, not weeks, and that is the point. A clean wall line comes in a few weeks; a reliable freestanding hold is a 6 to 12 month arc. You are using that arc to build the body you need anyway.

Built around you

Your wrists set the pace. You have flagged them as your weak link and you load them all day as a bodyworker. So this programme is wrist-first: every session warms them thoroughly, load is added slowly, and two of your six days are deliberately light (mobility and shape, almost no wrist loading). Put those light days the morning before your heaviest client days. A practice that eats into your work has failed, not succeeded.

What you already have going for you: you are comfortable being upside down, so you skip the fear that stalls most beginners. And your body awareness from bodywork is half of balancing. Use it: feel where your weight sits in your hands.

A bodyworker's bonus. You know how to release a forearm. Fold 30 to 60 seconds of your own wrist-flexor and forearm release into the warm-up and again after, especially in the early weeks. It is prep and recovery you are already expert at.

The global guardrails

These apply in every phase. When in doubt, come down. Junk reps build junk patterns.

Wrists — sharp or joint pain

A muscular ache or stretch in the forearm is fine. Sharp pain, or pain in the wrist joint itself, is a stop. Come out, return to warm-up and prep-only loading, and reduce volume next session. If wrist soreness follows you into your work day, you did too much: cut the loaded work, do the light-day version, warm up longer.

Neck or arms — tingling, or pressure in the neck

You should feel this in your hands and shoulders, never a compressed neck or pins-and-needles down the arms. If you do, come down. It usually means weight is dumping into the wrong place or the shoulders are not pushing tall.

Shoulders — a pinch at the top of the reach

If reaching fully overhead pinches, do not force it. Reduce the range, spend more of the session on shoulder mobility, and let the range open over weeks. This is common when returning from a break.

Head rush or dizziness

Come out of every inversion slowly. If you feel light-headed on standing up, sit for a moment and hydrate. Never bolt upright out of a hold.

A pinch in the lower back upside down

That is the "banana": you are arching, not stacking. Ribs down, squeeze the glutes, think hollow. If it persists, you need more Phase 0 core, so drop back and build it. See the diagram below for the shape you are chasing.

The moment of panic when you tip over

Never kick up freestanding until you own your bail. That is the entire job of Phase 2. When you over-balance, you turn and cartwheel out to the side. Learn it first, drill it until it is automatic, and the fear never gets to build.

The warm-up — every session, about 4 minutes

Never load a cold wrist, and mornings you are coldest. This is not optional; it is the reason your wrists last.

  • Forearm release 30–60sYour speciality. Work the forearm flexors and the base of the palm before anything loads them.
  • Palms-down rocks ×10On all fours, hands flat, fingers forward. Rock weight forward over the wrists and back. Slow.
  • Fingers-back rocks ×10Hands flat, fingers pointing toward your knees, palms down. Gently rock back to stretch the underside. Ease into range.
  • Side-to-side rocks ×10Hands flat and fixed, sway side to side over the wrists to open the lateral range.
  • Knuckle & fingertip pulses ×10–15Push into the fingertips and lift the palm slightly, like the balance grip you will use later. Wakes up the hands.
  • Shoulder opener ~45sWall slides, or arm circles, or thread-the-needle. Get the overhead reach moving.
  • Hollow activation 2×10sA quick tucked hollow hold to switch the core on before the main work.

The shape you are chasing

The four phases

Tap a phase to open it. Do only the phase you are on.

0 FoundationsWrists, line and core · typically 2–4 weeks ▸

Goal: earn the right to be upside down. Build wrist tolerance, a flat overhead line, and a real hollow-body core, before any meaningful inversions. Nothing here is scary; it is the foundation everything else stands on.

The work (skill days)

  • Loaded wrist rocks 3×20sThe warm-up rocks, but now pause and hold at the end range under your bodyweight. Build the tissue tolerance. Stop at sharp pain.
  • Quadruped weight shift 3×30sOn all fours, hands under shoulders, shift weight forward onto the hands and hold. This is the first real wrist load. Progress by leaning further over time.
  • Hollow-body hold 4×20–30sLower back pressed flat to the floor. Start tucked (knees and arms in); extend the limbs only as far as you can keep the back flat. See the video.
  • Overhead wall reach 3×20sBack to a wall, arms reach overhead to touch it without the lower back arching off or elbows bending. This is your shoulder line.
  • Wall-walk intro (optional) 3 repsFeet on the wall, hands walk out to a plank, and back. A gentle taste of load through the shoulders. Only when the above feel easy.

1 Chest-to-wallThe line, and the shape of everything · 4–8+ weeks ▸

Goal: the single most valuable phase. Facing the wall forces the correct stacked, hollow line, because the wall will not let you arch away from it. This is where the strength and the shape are built. Do not rush past it, even when it feels repetitive.

The work (skill days)

  • Wall walk to chest-to-wall 3–5 repsFrom a plank with feet at the base of the wall, walk hands in and feet up until your chest and hips are close to the wall and stacked over the hands.
  • Chest-to-wall hold build to 60s totalThe main event. Push tall through the shoulders (ears "disappear" between the arms), ribs down, glutes on, squeeze everything long. Accumulate time in as many sets as it takes.
  • Shoulder shrugs in the hold 2×5In the chest-to-wall hold, shrug the shoulders up toward the ears and back down. Builds the active push you will balance on later.
  • One-foot taps (late in phase) 3×3 eachIn the hold, peel one foot off the wall a few centimetres, hold, replace. Your first taste of finding your own balance.

Watch the shoulders here

The commonest fault is letting the shoulders sink so the head pokes forward. Cue: push the floor away, make yourself tall, hide your ears between your arms. If the shoulder reach is not there yet, spend more time on the Phase 0 overhead work.

2 Kick-up & bailGetting in, and safely out · 4–8+ weeks ▸

Goal: learn to enter a freestanding handstand and, first and most importantly, to exit one. You learn the bail before you chase balance. Own the exit and the fear never forms.

The bail comes first

Spend the first week or two of this phase on the cartwheel bail alone until it is automatic in both directions. Only then start the freestanding kick-ups. This order is the whole safety model.

The work (skill days)

  • Cartwheel bail drill 5 each sideFrom a kick-up, when you feel yourself tipping over, turn the shoulders and step one hand round to cartwheel out to the side, feet landing wide. Practise it deliberately, not just when you panic.
  • Back-to-wall kick-up 5–8 repsLunge, hands down a hand-span from the wall, kick the back leg up and let the heels find the wall softly. Arrive gently; do not slam. This teaches the kick calibration.
  • Kick-up to balance (off wall) 5–8 repsIn open space, kick up and try to catch a moment of stillness, bailing out the side the instant it drifts. Every attempt ends in a controlled exit, never a crash.

3 FreestandingTurning the float into a hold · ongoing, months ▸

Goal: extend that flash of balance into a hold, using the fingers and wrists to steer. There is no gate at the end of this phase, because this phase is the destination. It keeps giving for years.

The work (skill days)

  • Toe pulls 5–8 repsFrom chest-to-wall with toes touching, push tall, ribs tucked, and lean the shoulders forward just enough to peel the toes off and float. The core skill of finding balance. See the video.
  • Fingertip balance drills within holdsTipping forward (toward the fingers)? Press hard into the fingertips and the knuckles to push yourself back. Tipping back (toward the heels of the hand)? Ease the fingers. This is the steering wheel.
  • Freestanding kick-up to hold 6–10 repsKick up and stay, steering with the hands, bailing the instant it runs away. Quality over quantity; stop when the shape falls apart.
  • Heel-pull drill 3–5 repsFor over-balance: from chest-to-wall, pull the heels off and down toward the floor behind you, learning to arrest a forward tip.

Where to go from here

Once a 30-second hold is reliable, you have arrived, and you have also built exactly the wrists, shoulders, core and back you set out to build. Natural next steps in calisthenics: the handstand press (from a pike or straddle), the tuck planche, the L-sit, and shoulder-taps in the handstand. Same principles: warm the wrists, build slowly, own the bail.
