The Handstand Project

A wall-based progression to a freestanding handstand. About 20 minutes, six mornings a week, over months.

6 days + 1 rest ~20 min 4 phases

Each session: warm-up, your current phase, then a short strength finisher. Advance a phase only when you pass its test. Ticks save on this device.

Guardrails

Wrists: sharp or joint pain

Forearm ache is fine. Sharp pain, or pain in the joint, means stop. Go back to the warm-up and prep-only loading, and cut volume next session.

Neck or arms: tingling, or pressure in the neck

You should feel it in the hands and shoulders. Tingling down the arms or a compressed neck means come down.

Shoulders: pinch at the top of the reach

Do not force it. Reduce the range and do more shoulder mobility. It opens over weeks.

Head rush or dizziness

Come out of every inversion slowly. Sit and hydrate if light-headed.

Pinch in the lower back upside down

You are arching (the banana). Ribs down, glutes on, more hollow work.

Fear when tipping over

Never kick up freestanding until the cartwheel bail is automatic. That is the job of Phase 2.

Warm-up · every session · ~5 min

Do not load cold wrists.

  • Forearm & shoulder release 30–60sFree up the forearm flexors and shoulders.
  • Wrist rocks, all directions 3×10On all fours: palms-down rock forward, fingers-back rock, then side to side. Slow.
  • Knuckle & fingertip pulses ×10–15Push into the fingertips and lift the palm slightly.
  • Shoulder circles or wall slides ~45sMove the overhead reach through full range.
  • Thread-the-needle or band pass-throughs ~45sOpen the shoulders towards overhead. No pinching.
  • Scapular push-ups ×8In a plank, spread the shoulder blades, then squeeze.
  • Hollow hold 2×10sTucked, lower back flat.

Alignment

The aim is to stack the big joints, wrists under shoulders under hips under ankles, so you balance with little effort instead of muscling it. The common fault is arching the lower back (the banana), which pushes the hips out of the stack and loads the spine. What each part does, from the ground up:

  • HandsFingers spread, gripping the floor, weight through the fingertips.
  • ShouldersPush tall and reach fully overhead, ears between the arms. This is what stacks you.
  • RibsDo not let your ribcage pop up and forwards. Gently brace your stomach so the front of your body stays flat. Same feeling as the hollow-body hold.
  • HipsDirectly over your shoulders, not your backside sticking out behind you. Tuck your tailbone slightly under, the way you would to flatten your lower back on the floor.
  • LegsGlutes squeezed, legs together and straight, toes pointed.

The phases

Do only the phase you are on.

0 FoundationsWrists, shoulders, core · 2–4 weeks ▸

Goal: build wrist tolerance, open shoulders overhead, and a hollow-body core before inverting.

The work

  • Loaded wrist rocks 3×20sPause and hold at end range under bodyweight. Stop at sharp pain.
  • Quadruped weight shift 3×30sOn all fours, shift weight forward onto the hands and hold. Lean further over time.
  • Hollow-body hold 4×20–30sLower back flat. Start tucked; extend the limbs only while the back stays flat.
  • Overhead wall reach 3×20sBack to a wall, arms overhead to touch it without arching the back or bending the elbows.
  • Wall-walk intro (optional) 3 repsFeet on the wall, walk hands out to a plank and back.

Ready for Phase 1

1 Chest-to-wallThe line and its endurance · 4–8+ weeks ▸

Goal: build the stacked, hollow line and the endurance it needs. The most important phase; do not rush it.

The work

  • Wall walk to chest-to-wall 3–5 repsFrom a plank with feet at the wall, walk hands in and feet up until chest and hips stack over the hands.
  • Chest-to-wall hold build to 60sPush tall through the shoulders, ribs down, glutes on, body long. Accumulate the time across sets.
  • Shoulder shrugs in the hold 2×5Shrug up towards the ears and back down.
  • One-foot taps (late) 3×3 eachPeel one foot off the wall a few centimetres, hold, replace.

Ready for Phase 2

2 Kick-up & bailIn, and safely out · 4–8+ weeks ▸

Goal: learn to enter a freestanding handstand, and to exit it. Learn the bail first.

Bail first

Drill the cartwheel bail until it is automatic in both directions before any freestanding kick-ups.

The work

  • Cartwheel bail drill 5 each sideWhen tipping past balance, turn the shoulders and cartwheel out to the side. Drill it deliberately.
  • Back-to-wall kick-up 5–8 repsLunge, hands a hand-span from the wall, kick up so the heels meet the wall softly.
  • Kick-up to balance (off wall) 5–8 repsIn open space, kick up, catch a moment of balance, bail out the side when it drifts.

Ready for Phase 3

3 FreestandingThe hold · ongoing ▸

Goal: extend the balance into a hold, steering with the fingers.

The work

  • Toe pulls 5–8 repsFrom chest-to-wall with toes touching, push tall, ribs tucked, lean the shoulders forward to float the toes off.
  • Fingertip balance within holdsTipping towards the fingers: press into the fingertips and knuckles. Tipping back: ease the fingers.
  • Freestanding kick-up to hold 6–10 repsKick up and stay, steering with the hands. Bail when it runs away.
  • Heel-pull drill 3–5 repsFrom chest-to-wall, pull the heels off and down towards the floor to arrest a forward tip.

Milestones

Strength

The phases teach balance; this builds the shoulders, back and core underneath it, and is your on-ramp to calisthenics. On skill days, after the balance work, do one push exercise and one or two core exercises. About 5 to 8 minutes.

Push

  • Push-ups 3 setsFull range, chest to the floor. When 3×12 is easy, raise the feet onto a step.
  • Pike push-ups 3×5–10Hips high in an inverted V, lower the head towards the floor between the hands. The main builder of overhead pressing strength, and the path to a handstand push-up later. Raise the feet higher over time.

Core, both sides

  • Hollow hold 3×20–40sThe front. Lie on your back, lower back pressed flat, shoulders and legs lifted. Progress to slow hollow rocks.
  • Arch / superman hold 3×20–30sThe back, the half most people skip. Face down, lift the chest and legs off the floor. This is where your back gets stronger.
  • Leg raises 3×8–12Lying, lift straight legs to vertical and lower slowly. Builds towards a tuck L-sit.

Pull · optional, needs a bar or rings

  • Rows or pull-ups 3 setsNot needed for the handstand itself, but it balances the shoulders and posture and makes this a complete calisthenics base. Add it if you have a bar.
